# Hawaii WARN source transition: release blocker

The current Hawaii collector failed on September 6, 2026 while trying to fetch a relative Cloudflare email-protection link as a URL. The failure is reproducible from its upstream code: it walks every anchor under `div#container_main` as though each were a yearly WARN page. This also misses a more important source change. The [official historical index](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wdc/real-time-warn-updates/) says notices as of **August 1, 2026** moved to the [Workforce Development Division's current notice page](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wdd/warn-notices/).

The current page listed five notices when captured: Amentum, Festival Fun Parks, Aliʻi Health Center, The Evangelical Lutheran Good Samaritan Society, and First Insurance Company of Hawaii. Each links to a notice detail page. The [Amentum detail](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wdd/warn-notices/warn-notice-amentum/) separately labels **Date Department Received WARN** as August 20, 2026 and **Layoff Effective** as October 31, 2026, and links the filing PDF. The agency receipt day is not automatically the employer's legal notice day. The old collector and normalizer were built for the historical yearly list and must not silently map the new detail page's receipt day into canonical notice date.

The historical index still links yearly pages for 2019–2026. Its [2026 archive page](https://labor.hawaii.gov/wdc/2026-warn-notices/) reaches July 31 and includes updates and rescissions that need event interpretation. The current page starts in August. The first four captured HTML artifacts and hashes are saved in [the transition manifest](/Users/brendanchapuis/Projects/warn-live/data/source_snapshots/hi/2026-09-23-transition/manifest.json). This is a diagnostic source capture, not yet part of the reviewed rebuild bundle.

**Release disposition:** mark live Hawaii collection broken until a replacement adapter reads only the identified yearly WARN links, adds the current WDD detail pages, preserves receipt and effective dates in their distinct roles, and reconciles updates/rescissions without guessing event identity. Verify the resulting raw-row counts and source correspondence against a fresh official capture before admitting new rows. The v10 historical Hawaii candidate remains an older source snapshot; it is not evidence of current collection health.

## Conservative adapter phase

An inactive `warnlive.fetch.custom.hi` collector and `warnlive.normalize.custom.hi` parser now filter official archive/WDD links and keep WDD receipt apart from legal notice. The fetcher replaces its raw CSV only after every discovered page succeeds. The pinned archive and Amentum diagnostic pages pass focused tests; Amentum's labeled October 31 layoff day and 62 workers are projected, while its August 20 agency receipt remains only in source details. Transition headcounts and on-or-after dates are not turned into exact layoffs or starts.

The pinned 2026 archive exposed two Kauai Coffee rows where the only linked PDF is a later rescission document. The parser now retains **Kauai Coffee Company, LLC** as the listed employer for both January 12 and March 6, records the link as a rescission attachment, and keeps the archive listing day separate from canonical legal notice. It likewise preserves the January 26 DFS update text without inferring an event link. Qualified WDD dates such as “on or after” and multi-day spans are not reduced to exact effective days.

This phase is **not active in `states.yaml`**. It has no full live crawl or filing-level update/rescission correspondence. The source-identity and old-key migration boundaries also need a guarded integration before activation. A read-only regression across the frozen source bundle's `raw/hi.csv` and `backfill/raw/hi.csv` found that the new normalizer produces exactly the same 453 accepted records and 18 failures per file as the legacy normalizer, including identical canonical fields and failure details. Thus this phase does not change the current source-only candidate, but it does not clear the HI health blocker.
