# Illinois IEBS date roles and fresh source (2026-09-23)

This review uses the public [Illinois IEBS event-entry guide](https://www.illinoisworknet.com/DownloadPrint/IEBS%20Guide%20-%20Event%20Creation.pdf) (pinned as [`guide.pdf`](../data/source_snapshots/il/2026-09-23/guide.pdf)) and two captured public exports. The guide says a layoff's **Report date** defaults to the day of entry and may be changed for an earlier notice, while `WARN Received`, `Supplemental Received`, `Last Reported`, and `Initial Date Reported` are distinct search filters. Its notification phase is populated when a layoff notification is entered into IEBS. Those labels do not establish when affected employees received legal WARN notice. The upstream Illinois transformer nevertheless put `Initial Date Reported` into canonical `notice_date`. That is an unsupported date role for notice-to-layoff timing.

The normalization rule now leaves Illinois `notice_date` unknown. It keeps `Initial Date Reported` as `agency_reported_date`, `Last Report Date` as a separate agency update, and each comma-separated `Notification Date(s)` component as an agency notification activity date, preserving source text and malformed tokens. It does not choose an earliest or latest notification as employee notice. IEBS ID is an opaque source-record identity, never a date. The DCEO monthly effective-date repair continues to match its `notified` day against the *agency report* anchor while filling only missing effective dates; it no longer requires a populated legal notice day. That matching is a source correspondence heuristic, not proof that employees received notice on the report day. Source-health freshness uses the agency report date, so the legal-date correction does not mask a stale feed.

## Current public capture

The September 23 Eastern-time public IEBS export yielded **4,890 rows and 4,890 unique nonblank IEBS IDs**, with zero parse failures and a passing source verification. Its newest normalized report day was September 16, seven days before the probe. The source workbook, derived CSV, exact API URL, hashes, and guide are pinned in the [Illinois capture manifest](../data/source_snapshots/il/2026-09-23/manifest.json). Against the prior 4,871-row frozen export, all old IEBS IDs remain and exactly **19 IDs are added**. Those are precisely the 19 old-local-database IDs absent from the earlier candidate. Nine existing IDs have revised raw cells. For example, Norvax IEBS `20251104001` now reports `Norvax, LLC/GoHealth, LLC`, a September 9, 2026 agency notification entry, and 598 revised layoffs; the earlier capture reported `Norvax, LLC`, November 3, 2025, and 487 revised layoffs. The `Approximate Total # of Full-Time Employees` field remains 487 in both. The source changes are one IEBS record's chronology, not two worker counts to add together. Several other revised rows append agency notification dates while keeping the original `Initial Date Reported`; none warrants overwriting a legal employee notice date because the export does not supply one.

## Interpretation and release boundary

The current export resolves the *capture freshness* part of the Illinois coverage gap, but it does not establish legal notice dates. The old local database's Illinois date field and any notice-to-layoff calculation using it need relabeling or exclusion. Distinct IEBS IDs are distinct source records; a further filing/event relationship check is needed for rows that may describe the same layoff. An initial diagnostic replay revealed that nulling Illinois notice dates would otherwise re-admit thousands of older Big Local News rows under content keys. That candidate was rejected; source-only rebuilding now holds Illinois BLN rows with `source_identity_unresolved`, matching the existing receipt-date-state boundary. Their historical coverage remains a separate limit. An isolated guarded date-role replay and timing/export comparison must pass before this bundle can become a release candidate; the checked-in database and site have not been replaced.
